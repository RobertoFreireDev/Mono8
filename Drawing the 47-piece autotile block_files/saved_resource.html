Drawing the 47-piece autotile block

Forty-seven tiles sounds like forty-seven drawings. It is four, rotated — every piece in the block is just its four corners, and each corner has only five things it can be.

Every tile below is already on your sheet: this exact art now sits in block (3,0), sprites 024–191, marked as an autotile in data.atl. Draw over it — the shapes are the point, not the two greens.

What decides a piece

A cell either holds terrain or it does not. Which of the forty-seven pieces it shows is read off the eight cells around it: the four cardinals always, and each diagonal only when both of the cardinals beside it hold terrain too — otherwise the gap between them has already cut that corner off. Exactly forty-seven neighbourhoods survive that rule, which is why the block holds forty-seven pieces and not two hundred and fifty-six.

You never pick a piece by hand. You paint terrain, and the brush picks.

Two of those five are the same shape turned ninety degrees, so the real count is four drawings: an outer corner, a straight edge, an inner corner, and plain interior. Draw them once for the top-left quadrant, then use the sprite editor's R to rotate and F / V to flip for the other three.

The bright rim is the lit edge — whatever your terrain does where it meets open air. Keep it to the outermost pixel and the pieces will tile without seams.

The first cell stays blank. Cell 0 of every block is its empty tile — the one the brush stamps to erase — so sprite 024 must be left empty even though nothing stops you drawing in it. Anything you put there will be painted onto the map wherever you erase. (In block (0,0) the empty cell is sprite 000, which the sheet keeps blank for you.)

Doing it in the editor

  1. Start at the last cell

    Sprite 191 is plain interior — no edges, no corners, just the terrain's fill and whatever texture it carries. Everything else is that tile with corners taken out of it, so it sets the palette for the other forty-six.

  2. Turn the guide on

    The button between the paint tools and Select toggles the autotile guide. It overlays the 3×3 diagram from Fig. 2 straight onto your canvas, so you can read which neighbours a cell is drawn for without counting rows. It is blue until the block is marked, green after.

  3. Draw one corner, then rotate

    Sprite 025 — the lone cell — is all four outer corners at once, so drawing it gives you that shape in every orientation. Copy quadrants out of it with R, F and V rather than redrawing them, and the block stays consistent.

  4. Mark the block, then paint

    In the Map Editor, select any sprite of the block and hit the autotile button — AUTOTILE ON. From then on the pencil lays terrain instead of stamping sprites, and the block's top-left sprite erases it again.

  5. Flag all forty-seven

    Collision reads sprite flags, and the brush is free to swap a cell for any other piece as its neighbours change — so set flag 1 on every one of the forty-seven, and leave it off cell 0. Miss one and the terrain will be solid in some shapes and walk-through in others.
